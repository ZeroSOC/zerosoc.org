5 November 2026 Working Group

Third Working Group call: verdicts, governance and when an incident ends

The ZeroSOC Working Group met online on Thursday 5 November for its third call, with two members joining for the first time. We covered the OCSF extension, how the project will be governed, where a near-miss ends and an incident begins, and when response time should stop counting. We closed with a live demo.

OCSF reserved UID 984 for ZeroSOC

Our request was merged into the official OCSF registry, so UID 984 now belongs to the ZeroSOC extension. The extension adds a field only where OCSF has no concept of its own, and Cases are correlated through OCSF's correlation_uid. Staying close to the public schema also pays off for AI agents: a model that already knows OCSF can read ZeroSOC records without extra prompting.

From one maintainer to community governance

The project is moving to an open, community-governed model. The proposed rules for changes:

  • Everyday changes (editorial fixes, drafts, edits to development documents) can be merged by a maintainer once the automated checks are green.
  • Decisions (design decisions on development or stable documents, governance changes) start with an issue, then a pull request that stays open for 72 hours with every objection addressed.
  • Promotion to stable, when a document becomes binding for conformance, needs maturity checks and a review by someone other than the author.

We are looking for maintainers: people who review pull requests, help settle disagreements and cut releases. If you would like to take this on, tell us on Slack.

Near-miss or incident?

The group discussed a single threshold question: did the malicious activity reach the environment? If it did and can cause harm, it is an Incident and needs a response. If it was stopped before, at a mail or web gateway or inside a sandbox, it is a Near-miss: the Case closes without remediation and stays on record. Both carry the True Positive verdict; severity tells them apart.

One participant proposed giving False Positives and Benign Positives severity_id 1 as well, and keeping 2 to 5 for Incidents only. An automated triage then has one simple rule: anything above Informational is a real incident. We also want a decision matrix that draws the boundaries between near-miss, impact and benign activity more formally.

Early containment and where MTTR stops

Should High and Critical Cases allow containment before the Incident is declared, while the investigation is still testing its hypotheses? The idea on the table is bounded: only pre-authorised, reversible actions, such as a conditional access change or isolating a device, instead of waiting for the formal declaration.

We then walked through the seven operational gates of a Case:

  • G1 Received
  • G2 Triage disposition
  • G3 Incident declared
  • G4 First containment
  • G5 Eradication verified
  • G6 Recovery complete
  • G7 Case closed

The open question is where Mean Time to Respond stops: at G5 when the threat is eradicated, at G6 when services are restored and containment is lifted, or at G7 when the Case closes after the post-incident review. The industry has no shared definition of MTTD, triage time or MTTR, which is why published figures are so hard to compare.

Live demo of the reference platform

We closed with an end-to-end run of the open-source reference platform on Azure. It orchestrates its workflows with LangGraph and calls models through Azure AI Foundry, held in place by deterministic playbook guardrails. It is built in layers: the framework specifications, the agent skills, the MCP servers and the execution engine. Attacks are simulated in a cyber range on Azure VMs with Caldera and Atomic Red Team.

An Atomic Red Team scenario (a phishing macro launching PowerShell and further binaries) ran on a VM monitored by Microsoft Defender. ZeroSOC picked up the Defender alert, finished triage within a minute, correlated across the process trees within two, proposed isolating the device and posted the alert to Microsoft Teams. When new evidence arrived, it re-evaluated the Case, updated the verdict and kept the full audit trail.

The MCP servers and agent skills are already public. Active members who want to look at the platform code can ask for access.
